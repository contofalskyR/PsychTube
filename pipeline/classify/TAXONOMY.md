# Contested findings (ids) — tag a video only if it presents the finding as established fact, or if it critiques it
- `power-posing` — Power posing: The 2010 power-posing hormone and risk-taking effects did not replicate; co-author Dana Carney disavowed them in 2016. A smaller 'felt power' self-report effect may survive.
- `stanford-prison` — Stanford Prison Experiment: Archival recordings show guards were coached to be cruel and a key 'breakdown' was reportedly acted; the study is now widely seen as a demonstration, not an experiment.
- `marshmallow-test` — Marshmallow test: A 2018 conceptual replication with a larger, more diverse sample found the link between delay of gratification and later outcomes was much smaller and mostly explained by family background.
- `ego-depletion` — Ego depletion / willpower as a muscle: A 2016 multi-lab preregistered replication (23 labs) found an effect indistinguishable from zero.
- `social-priming` — Social priming (elderly-words walking, money priming): Classic behavioral priming studies have failed repeated high-powered replications; Kahneman himself urged the field to clean up its act in 2012.
- `facial-feedback` — Facial feedback (pen-in-teeth): A 17-lab registered replication found no effect of the pen-in-mouth smile manipulation on humor ratings, though later meta-analyses find small effects under some conditions.
- `growth-mindset` — Growth mindset interventions: Effects on achievement are real but small (d ≈ 0.1) and concentrated among lower-achieving students; early claims of large effects were overstated.
- `learning-styles` — Learning styles: No credible evidence that matching teaching to a student's 'learning style' improves learning; it is considered a neuromyth.
- `10000-hours` — 10,000-hour rule: Deliberate practice explains a minority of the variance in expert performance (about 1–26% depending on domain); the round number is Gladwell's, not Ericsson's finding.
- `grit` — Grit: Grit overlaps heavily with conscientiousness and predicts outcomes only modestly beyond it; the original claims were larger than meta-analytic estimates.
- `left-right-brain` — Left-brained / right-brained people: Hemispheric specialization is real, but there is no evidence people are dominated by one hemisphere in personality or thinking style.
- `milgram-nuance` — Milgram obedience (nuance): The headline 65% figure comes from one of ~24 conditions; archival work shows many participants disbelieved the shocks were real, and compliance varied widely across variants. The core effect is not debunked.
- `bystander-genovese` — Kitty Genovese / 38 witnesses story: The '38 witnesses who did nothing' account was largely a New York Times myth; some neighbors did call police or shout. The bystander effect itself is a real but context-dependent finding.
- `dunning-kruger-artifact` — Dunning-Kruger effect (debate): Part of the classic pattern can be reproduced from random noise plus regression to the mean; the size and interpretation of the effect are debated, though the pattern is not purely artifactual.
- `implicit-bias-iat` — Implicit Association Test as a predictor: The IAT has low test-retest reliability and weakly predicts discriminatory behavior; its creators now describe it as unsuited for diagnosing individuals.
- `mozart-effect` — Mozart effect: Listening to Mozart does not make you smarter; the original brief spatial-reasoning effect is small, short-lived and reproducible with any arousing music.
- `little-albert` — Little Albert: Watson's conditioning demonstration had one subject, no controls, and the baby's identity and health are disputed; it is a historical landmark rather than solid evidence.
- `serotonin-depression` — Chemical imbalance theory of depression: A 2022 umbrella review found no consistent evidence that depression is caused by low serotonin; antidepressants can still work without that explanation being true.
- `stereotype-threat` — Stereotype threat (women and math): Large preregistered replications found little or no stereotype-threat effect on women's math performance; publication bias inflated the early literature.
- `mirror-neurons` — Mirror neurons explain empathy/autism: Mirror neurons exist in monkeys, but the 'broken mirror' theory of autism and claims that they explain empathy or language are not supported.
- `huberman-protocols` — Huberman protocols (overreach): Popular mechanistic protocols (sunlight timing, cold exposure, supplements) often extrapolate from small or animal studies; the physiology is real, the certainty is not.
- `nofap-porn` — Porn 'addiction' as brain disease: Claims that porn rewires the brain like a drug and causes erectile dysfunction come largely from advocacy, not controlled research; evidence for 'porn-induced ED' is weak and moral disapproval predicts self-reported 'addiction' better than use does.
- `amy-cuddy-ted` — TED-style single-study claims: Many TED talks present a single study as settled science; several of the most-watched psychology talks rest on effects that later shrank or vanished.
- `mbti` — Myers-Briggs (MBTI): The MBTI has poor test-retest reliability and its dichotomies do not match how traits are distributed; personality psychologists use the Big Five instead.
- `rat-park` — Rat Park: Rat Park's message (environment matters for addiction) is supported, but the original study was small, had confounds, and direct replications were mixed; it is not proof that addiction is 'just' about disconnection.
- `multiple-intelligences` — Multiple intelligences: Gardner's intelligences lack empirical support as separate abilities; measured cognitive abilities correlate strongly (the g factor).
- `polyvagal` — Polyvagal theory: Popular in trauma therapy, but its core physiological claims about the vagus nerve are contradicted by comparative neuroanatomy.
- `body-keeps-score` — 'Body keeps the score' / repressed memory claims: Trauma's effects are real, but repressed-then-recovered memory claims lack solid support and contributed to false accusations; memory researchers dispute 'body memory' framings.
- `attachment-styles-pop` — Adult 'attachment styles' as fixed types: Attachment is continuous and changes over time; pop versions treating anxious/avoidant as fixed personality types overstate the research.
- `dopamine-detox` — Dopamine detox / 'dopamine fasting': You cannot deplete or reset dopamine by avoiding pleasures; the useful part (reducing compulsive habits) has nothing to do with dopamine levels.
