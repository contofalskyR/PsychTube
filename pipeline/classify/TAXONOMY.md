# Contested findings (ids) — tag a video only if it presents the finding as established fact, or if it critiques it

Each id carries the status PsychTube shows. Findings that **held up** are listed too (Stroop, McGurk, rubber hand,
inattentional blindness, split-brain, Asch, Robbers Cave, Pavlov…): tag a video that presents one of them as well,
so it gets the reassuring chip. `contested_role` is `promotes` when the video presents the finding, `critique` when it
pushes back on it (a lecture that teaches a classic while explaining its problems counts as `critique`).

- `power-posing` — Power posing [Did not replicate]: The 2010 power-posing hormone and risk-taking effects did not replicate; co-author Dana Carney disavowed them in 2016. A smaller 'felt power' self-report effect may survive.
- `stanford-prison` — Stanford Prison Experiment [Misreported]: Archival recordings show guards were coached to be cruel and a key 'breakdown' was reportedly acted; the study is now widely seen as a demonstration, not an experiment.
- `marshmallow-test` — Marshmallow test [Smaller than claimed]: A 2018 conceptual replication with a larger, more diverse sample found the link between delay of gratification and later outcomes was much smaller and mostly explained by family background.
- `ego-depletion` — Ego depletion / willpower as a muscle [Did not replicate]: A 2016 multi-lab preregistered replication (23 labs) found an effect indistinguishable from zero.
- `social-priming` — Social priming (elderly-words walking, money priming) [Did not replicate]: Classic behavioral priming studies have failed repeated high-powered replications; Kahneman himself urged the field to clean up its act in 2012.
- `facial-feedback` — Facial feedback (pen-in-teeth) [Mixed evidence]: A 17-lab registered replication found no effect of the pen-in-mouth smile manipulation on humor ratings, though later meta-analyses find small effects under some conditions.
- `growth-mindset` — Growth mindset interventions [Smaller than claimed]: Effects on achievement are real but small (d ≈ 0.1) and concentrated among lower-achieving students; early claims of large effects were overstated.
- `learning-styles` — Learning styles [Myth]: No credible evidence that matching teaching to a student's 'learning style' improves learning; it is considered a neuromyth.
- `10000-hours` — 10,000-hour rule [Overstated]: Deliberate practice explains a minority of the variance in expert performance (about 1–26% depending on domain); the round number is Gladwell's, not Ericsson's finding.
- `grit` — Grit [Overstated]: Grit overlaps heavily with conscientiousness and predicts outcomes only modestly beyond it; the original claims were larger than meta-analytic estimates.
- `left-right-brain` — Left-brained / right-brained people [Myth]: Hemispheric specialization is real, but there is no evidence people are dominated by one hemisphere in personality or thinking style.
- `milgram-nuance` — Milgram obedience (nuance) [Mixed evidence]: The headline 65% figure comes from one of ~24 conditions; archival work shows many participants disbelieved the shocks were real, and compliance varied widely across variants. The core effect is not debunked.
- `bystander-genovese` — Kitty Genovese / 38 witnesses story [Misreported]: The '38 witnesses who did nothing' account was largely a New York Times myth; some neighbors did call police or shout. The bystander effect itself is a real but context-dependent finding.
- `dunning-kruger-artifact` — Dunning-Kruger effect (debate) [Mixed evidence]: Part of the classic pattern can be reproduced from random noise plus regression to the mean; the size and interpretation of the effect are debated, though the pattern is not purely artifactual.
- `implicit-bias-iat` — Implicit Association Test as a predictor [Overstated]: The IAT has low test-retest reliability and weakly predicts discriminatory behavior; its creators now describe it as unsuited for diagnosing individuals.
- `mozart-effect` — Mozart effect [Did not replicate]: Listening to Mozart does not make you smarter; the original brief spatial-reasoning effect is small, short-lived and reproducible with any arousing music.
- `little-albert` — Little Albert [Misreported]: Watson's conditioning demonstration had one subject, no controls, and the baby's identity and health are disputed; it is a historical landmark rather than solid evidence.
- `serotonin-depression` — Chemical imbalance theory of depression [Overstated]: A 2022 umbrella review found no consistent evidence that depression is caused by low serotonin; antidepressants can still work without that explanation being true.
- `stereotype-threat` — Stereotype threat (women and math) [Did not replicate]: Large preregistered replications found little or no stereotype-threat effect on women's math performance; publication bias inflated the early literature.
- `mirror-neurons` — Mirror neurons explain empathy/autism [Overstated]: Mirror neurons exist in monkeys, but the 'broken mirror' theory of autism and claims that they explain empathy or language are not supported.
- `huberman-protocols` — Huberman protocols (overreach) [Overstated]: Popular mechanistic protocols (sunlight timing, cold exposure, supplements) often extrapolate from small or animal studies; the physiology is real, the certainty is not.
- `nofap-porn` — Porn 'addiction' as brain disease [Overstated]: Claims that porn rewires the brain like a drug and causes erectile dysfunction come largely from advocacy, not controlled research; evidence for 'porn-induced ED' is weak and moral disapproval predicts self-reported 'addiction' better than use does.
- `amy-cuddy-ted` — TED-style single-study claims [Overstated]: Many TED talks present a single study as settled science; several of the most-watched psychology talks rest on effects that later shrank or vanished.
- `mbti` — Myers-Briggs (MBTI) [Overstated]: The MBTI has poor test-retest reliability and its dichotomies do not match how traits are distributed; personality psychologists use the Big Five instead.
- `rat-park` — Rat Park [Mixed evidence]: Rat Park's message (environment matters for addiction) is supported, but the original study was small, had confounds, and direct replications were mixed; it is not proof that addiction is 'just' about disconnection.
- `multiple-intelligences` — Multiple intelligences [Did not replicate]: Gardner's intelligences lack empirical support as separate abilities; measured cognitive abilities correlate strongly (the g factor).
- `polyvagal` — Polyvagal theory [Overstated]: Popular in trauma therapy, but its core physiological claims about the vagus nerve are contradicted by comparative neuroanatomy.
- `body-keeps-score` — 'Body keeps the score' / repressed memory claims [Overstated]: Trauma's effects are real, but repressed-then-recovered memory claims lack solid support and contributed to false accusations; memory researchers dispute 'body memory' framings.
- `attachment-styles-pop` — Adult 'attachment styles' as fixed types [Overstated]: Attachment is continuous and changes over time; pop versions treating anxious/avoidant as fixed personality types overstate the research.
- `dopamine-detox` — Dopamine detox / 'dopamine fasting' [Myth]: You cannot deplete or reset dopamine by avoiding pleasures; the useful part (reducing compulsive habits) has nothing to do with dopamine levels.
- `choice-overload` — Choice overload / paradox of choice [Smaller than claimed]: The famous jam-study effect (more options, fewer purchases) averages close to zero across studies; choice overload appears only under specific conditions such as time pressure or unfamiliar options.
- `goal-announcing` — Announcing goals makes you less likely to achieve them [Did not replicate]: Rests on a few small studies by Gollwitzer and colleagues; later work found sharing goals with people whose opinion you value can increase commitment and performance.
- `stress-mindset` — Stress mindset (‘make stress your friend’) [Overstated]: Based mainly on an observational study where perceived stress predicted mortality only when people also believed stress was harmful; causality and the size of the effect remain unsettled.
- `asch-conformity` — Asch conformity [Smaller than claimed]: The effect is real and has replicated for seventy years, but a meta-analysis of 133 studies found it shrank after the 1950s and is larger in collectivist cultures; most participants gave the right answer most of the time.
- `robbers-cave` — Robbers Cave [Misreported]: Robbers Cave (1954) was Sherif's second attempt: the 1953 Middle Grove camp had failed when the boys united against the staff, and was dropped from the record. The 1954 conflict was more staged than the textbooks say.
- `ape-language` — Apes learning language (Washoe, Koko, Nim, Kanzi) [Mixed evidence]: Terrace's analysis of Nim's signing found prompted, repetitive, non-syntactic strings, and Koko's claims were never fully documented; Kanzi's comprehension of spoken English is the strongest evidence. Apes learn symbols; whether they learn language depends on what you mean by language.
- `universal-emotions` — Universal facial expressions of basic emotion [Mixed evidence]: Ekman's six universal expressions rest partly on forced-choice methods; with free labelling and in remote groups, recognition is weaker and context-dependent (a Trobriand "fear face" was read as threat). Expressions are more variable than the basic-emotion view, though not arbitrary.
- `cognitive-dissonance` — Cognitive dissonance (induced compliance) [Mixed evidence]: Dissonance reduction is one of psychology's best-supported ideas, but a 39-lab registered replication of the classic induced-compliance paradigm (write a counter-attitudinal essay for little reward) found no reliable attitude change. The theory stands; the textbook demonstration is shakier.
- `anchoring` — Anchoring [Replicated]: One of the sturdiest effects in the field: Many Labs 1 replicated anchoring in all 36 samples across 12 countries, with effects as large as the originals.
- `framing-effect` — Gain–loss framing (the Asian disease problem) [Replicated]: Tversky and Kahneman's framing effect replicated across Many Labs 1 and 2 (dozens of sites, thousands of participants) with little variation between cultures.
- `bystander-effect` — The bystander effect [Mixed evidence]: In the lab, more bystanders means less and slower help, and a meta-analysis confirms it; but in 219 real CCTV-recorded conflicts in three countries, at least one bystander intervened 91% of the time, and more bystanders meant more chance of help.
- `macbeth-effect` — The Macbeth effect (moral cleansing) [Did not replicate]: The finding that recalling an unethical act makes people want to wash did not survive three direct replications, and later meta-analysis puts the effect near zero.
- `terror-management` — Terror management (mortality salience) [Did not replicate]: Many Labs 4 ran the flagship mortality-salience study (reminders of death make Americans favour pro-US essays) in 21 labs, with and without the original authors' involvement, and found no effect either way.
- `precognition-bem` — Bem's precognition ("Feeling the Future") [Did not replicate]: The 2011 JPSP paper claiming people can sense future events failed preregistered replications and became the case study that started the replication crisis.
- `hungry-judges` — Hungry judges [Misreported]: The famous drop in parole approvals before lunch is largely explained by how cases were scheduled (prisoners without lawyers came last in each session), not by judges' blood sugar; the implied effect size was never plausible.
- `oxytocin-trust` — Oxytocin, the "love hormone" [Overstated]: The intranasal-oxytocin studies behind the "trust hormone" story (Kosfeld 2005) have not replicated; a review of the literature found no reliable effect on trust in healthy adults, and most studies were underpowered.
- `brain-training` — Brain-training games make you smarter [Did not replicate]: People get better at the trained games; the gains do not transfer to untrained cognitive tasks or everyday functioning. A six-week trial of 11,000 people found no transfer, and a 2016 review found the industry's evidence did not meet basic standards.
- `bilingual-advantage` — The bilingual advantage in executive function [Mixed evidence]: Early studies reported sharper attention and inhibition in bilinguals; large samples and a meta-analysis correcting for publication bias find little or no advantage.
- `rosenhan` — Rosenhan's "On Being Sane in Insane Places" [Misreported]: Archival work found Rosenhan's own admission notes contradicted his account (he reported more symptoms than claimed), could not find most of the eight pseudopatients, and omitted one whose experience was positive. Asylum reform did not need the study, but it leaned on it.
- `subliminal-advertising` — Subliminal advertising ("Eat popcorn") [Misreported]: Vicary's 1957 cinema study never happened as described (he admitted it in 1962). Subliminal priming can nudge choices in tightly controlled lab conditions when people are already thirsty, which is a very different claim.
- `ten-percent-brain` — We only use 10% of our brains [Myth]: Imaging, lesion studies and metabolism all say otherwise: there is no dormant 90%, and damage almost anywhere costs something. The myth's origins are murky (misreadings of William James and early neuron counts).
- `hot-hand` — The hot-hand "fallacy" [Reversed]: The 1985 paper that declared basketball's hot hand an illusion contained a subtle selection bias; corrected, the original data show a real hot hand. A textbook case of the debunking being the thing that was wrong.
- `birth-order` — Birth order shapes personality [Did not replicate]: In three national samples totalling more than 20,000 people, birth order had no effect on the Big Five traits (a small first-born advantage in IQ remains).
- `pygmalion` — Teacher expectations (Pygmalion in the classroom) [Smaller than claimed]: Expectation effects on students exist but are small, mostly dissipate, and teacher expectations are mostly accurate; the dramatic 1968 claims came from a few odd data points.
- `mindfulness-hype` — Mindfulness as a cure-all [Overstated]: Meta-analysis finds moderate benefits for anxiety, depression and pain, comparable to other active treatments, and no good evidence for many broader claims; much of the literature is small, unblinded and poorly controlled.
- `microexpressions-lies` — Microexpressions reveal lies [Overstated]: Microexpressions are rare, appear in truth-tellers too, and human lie detection averages 54% in meta-analysis; training programs built on them have not shown reliable gains.
- `nlp-eye-movements` — Eye direction reveals lying (NLP) [Did not replicate]: The neuro-linguistic programming claim that liars look up and to the right failed three tests, including one with real high-stakes appeals.
- `media-violence` — Violent media causes aggression [Mixed evidence]: From the Bobo doll on, lab studies find short-term imitation; whether games and film raise real-world violence is disputed, with competing meta-analyses finding small effects or none once publication bias is corrected.
- `inattentional-blindness` — Inattentional blindness (the gorilla) [Replicated]: Holds up everywhere it has been tried: half of people miss the gorilla, expert radiologists miss one embedded in a CT scan, and knowing the trick does not protect you from the next unexpected event.
- `mcgurk-effect` — The McGurk effect [Replicated]: Forty years of replication: the illusion is robust, though its strength varies between individuals and languages (weaker in Japanese and Chinese listeners).
- `rubber-hand` — The rubber hand illusion [Mixed evidence]: The illusion replicates reliably and has an fMRI signature, but a 2020 study found that much of the self-report correlates with hypnotic suggestibility and expectations, so part of what people report may be demand characteristics.
- `stroop-effect` — The Stroop effect [Replicated]: Among the most reliable effects in experimental psychology, reproduced in essentially every sample since 1935; the argument is about mechanism, not existence.
- `split-brain-consciousness` — Split-brain patients have two minds [Mixed evidence]: The classic demonstrations are real, but a 2017 re-examination found split-brain patients could respond to stimuli anywhere in the visual field with either hand and verbally, suggesting one agent with divided perception rather than two separate conscious selves.
- `learned-helplessness` — Learned helplessness [Mixed evidence]: The dogs were real; the mechanism was backwards. Fifty years on, Maier and Seligman concluded that passivity in the face of shock is the default mammalian response, and what is learned is control — the opposite of the original story.
- `pavlov-bell` — Pavlov's bell [Misreported]: Pavlov used metronomes, buzzers, harmoniums and electric shocks far more than bells; the bell is largely a translation artefact. His dogs also did not simply "drool at a sound" — he was mapping the nervous system.
- `phineas-gage` — Phineas Gage, "no longer Gage" [Misreported]: The personality-change story rests on a few lines from one physician; Gage later held a skilled job as a stagecoach driver in Chile for years, which suggests substantial recovery that the textbooks leave out.
- `ariely-dishonesty` — Ariely's dishonesty research (honesty pledge, moral reminders) [Fraud or retraction]: The 2012 PNAS study showing that signing an honesty pledge at the top of a form reduces cheating was retracted in 2021 after analysts showed its insurance-company field data had been fabricated (who fabricated it is disputed); the authors' own 2020 replication had already found no effect. A 17-lab registered replication of the earlier 'moral reminder' (Ten Commandments) effect found none either. Talks presenting this research programme as established should be weighed accordingly.
